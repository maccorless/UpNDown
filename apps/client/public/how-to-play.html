How to Play UpNDown

UpNDown is a cooperative card game. Everyone wins together or loses together.

Quick start

Goal
Play every card from every hand onto the four piles. Win together or lose together.
A turn
Two piles go up from 1 and two go down from 100. Play a card higher than an up pile, or lower than a down pile. The exception: a card exactly 10 the other way is allowed and sets that pile back. Play the required minimum each turn (usually 2, or 1 once the draw pile is empty), then end your turn.
Solitaire or multiplayer
Solitaire is just you, works without a connection and saves on this device. Multiplayer is 2 to 6 players: host a table and share the code or link, or join a friend's.
Signing in
Optional. Sign in with Google to keep your name, settings and results. Play as a guest and nothing is kept.

The full rules follow.

Goal

Play all cards from all players onto four foundation piles.

Foundation Piles

  • Two ascending piles start at 1. You normally play higher cards.
  • Two descending piles start at 100. You normally play lower cards.

Special Rule: Backward 10

  • Ascending piles can also take a card exactly 10 lower than the top card.
  • Descending piles can also take a card exactly 10 higher than the top card.

Example: On ascending 47, you may play 57 (normal) or 37 (backward 10).

Turn Basics (Multiplayer)

  1. On your turn, play at least the required minimum cards.
  2. Play by selecting a card and then selecting a valid pile.
  3. Use End Turn only after meeting the minimum.

Undo

  • During your turn, you can Undo card plays one at a time.
  • Undo reverts the last card you played back to your hand.
  • Once you click End Turn, the undo history is cleared — no take-backs across turns.
  • Works in both multiplayer and solitaire.

Cheats

Cheats are off unless the game turns them on in Settings, under Cheat codes. Each one is set to Off, Once (one use per player per game) or Unlimited. Open the cheats panel by double tapping the UpNDown title, or with the Cheats button.

  • Card seeker (Ctrl/Cmd+P): type a card number to see where it is: in the draw pile, in a player’s hand, or already played. Only you see the answer. The other players are told that you used it, nothing more.
  • Hint: suggests the best play from what you can see (your hand, the piles, how many cards are left, and what teammates have marked) and gives up to three reasons you can check on the table. It lights up the card and the pile and says it in words. Only you see it. It also works in solitaire; with no connection it still gives the hint, without the reasons.
  • Skip turn (Ctrl/Cmd+S, after a confirmation): pass your turn without playing the minimum. Only on your turn, before you play a card. It cannot save you once you are stuck, so use it before then. If every player skips in a row and nobody plays a card, the game is lost.
  • Trade a card (Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+X, with a card selected): swap that card for the next one from the draw pile. The card you give goes back into the pile at a random place.
  • Other players are told when you skip, trade or use the card seeker. Skip and trade cannot be undone.
  • What you used is shown on the end screen and kept in your history if you are signed in.

Robots

  • In the lobby the host can add robots to fill seats. They count toward the number of players.
  • A robot plays by the same rules as you, using a trained player that sees what you see plus the list of cards already played. It never looks at hidden cards. Robots mark piles with reactions after every play, and read yours.
  • Robots only play while a person is at the table. If every person is away for 90 seconds, a game with robots ends.
  • Robots cannot be kicked, and no result is saved for them.

Joining a game in progress

  • The host presses Invite during a game to make a single-use link.
  • Whoever opens the link asks to join, and the host is asked to Let in or Decline.
  • If let in, the new player is dealt a hand from the draw pile at any time. Nobody else’s cards and no pile change.

Coming back to a game

  • If you close the page by mistake, the home page shows Rejoin game for each game still going on this device.
  • If you are signed in, opening Multiplayer puts you back in your game, from any device. With several games you choose which.
  • Where the site has a database, a game also survives the server restarting. If it shows that saving is unavailable, a game in progress may be lost on a restart.

Pile Preferences (Reactions)

  • When it is not your turn, you can signal interest in a pile by clicking your indicator.
  • Click to cycle: off → 👍 Like → ❤️ Love → 🔥 Really Love → off.
  • Each player has a small circle next to every pile (players 1–3 left, 4–6 right).
  • Reactions are visible to all players and persist until your turn starts.
  • Use these to hint that you have a great card for that pile!
  • A good way to use them: mark a pile by how close your best card for it is. Really love: a backward 10, or a card within about 2 steps of the top. Love: within about 5 steps. Like: within about 11 steps (these are the robots’ thresholds with the default 2 to 99 deck). Nothing otherwise.
  • If a teammate has marked a pile, avoid taking it with a big move. Only act on a mark that is current: after a card is played, update yours, because an old mark can mislead.

Turn Sequence

  • A row of numbered dots in the Players panel shows the turn order.
  • The green dot is the current player. Your dot has a blue border.
  • A label shows how many turns until yours — helpful for deciding when to react to piles.

Player Kick (Lobby)

  • The game host can remove players from the lobby by clicking the × button next to their name.
  • Kicked players receive a notification and are sent back to the home screen.

Solitaire Mode

  • Solitaire is great for practice and learning the pile logic.
  • Choosing Solitaire shows the game’s settings first, with Start Solitaire and Change settings. Settings are fixed once the game starts, so set up cheats, hand size and deck here; after a game ends, Change settings takes you back.
  • You still follow legal move rules and backward-10 plays.
  • There is no teammate turn order to coordinate, so play is faster.

Difficulty and Settings

You can tune game difficulty in Settings before a game starts.

  • Hand Size: 5 to 11 cards. Larger hands are usually easier because you have more choices. The deck must be big enough to deal every player a full hand.
  • Min Cards Per Turn: higher minimums are harder to win with.
  • Card Range: a smaller card range is usually easier to win.
  • Auto Refill Hand: turning this on generally makes the game easier.

Win and Loss

  • Win: all players clear all cards and draw pile is exhausted.
  • Loss: no legal moves remain for the active state.

Quick Strategy

  • Save backward-10 plays when possible — you’ll see a golden celebration when one lands!
  • Avoid pushing a pile too far if teammates may need lower or higher ranges.
  • Use pile reactions (👍 ❤️ 🔥) to silently communicate with teammates about which piles matter to you.
  • Check the turn sequence to see how many turns until yours — if you’re far away, react to piles so others know your intent.
  • Use Undo if you realize a play was suboptimal — but plan ahead, since you can’t undo after ending your turn.
